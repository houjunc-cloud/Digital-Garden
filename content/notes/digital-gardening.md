# A digital garden trades polish for revision

A blog is a stream: posts are dated, published once, and effectively frozen. A garden
is a graph: notes are undated in spirit, linked to each other, and rewritten whenever
the author learns something.

The trade is explicit. You give up the polish of a finished essay and get, in exchange,
the ability to publish an idea at 40% confidence and improve it in place. Most good
thinking dies in the gap between "interesting thought" and "essay I'd be willing to
defend" — the garden removes that gap as a publishing requirement.

## Why the maturity marker does the work

The hard part isn't writing notes, it's publishing unfinished ones without anxiety.
An explicit stage marker (🌱 / 🌿 / 🌳) solves this socially: a seedling note announces
its own tentativeness, so a reader can't reasonably hold it to essay standards, and
the author can't reasonably stall on it.

## Failure modes

- **The write-only garden.** Notes accumulate, nothing is ever revised, and it becomes
  a worse blog. The fix is a recurring review, not more capture.
- **Link theatre.** Links added for graph aesthetics rather than because the notes
  genuinely bear on each other. A link should be a claim that reading A helps you
  understand B.
- **Folder gravity.** Endlessly reorganising folders instead of writing. Search and
  links do the retrieval work; folders are just a rough sort.
